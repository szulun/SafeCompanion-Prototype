Recording every call, within California law

Every call is recorded only after everyone on it hears a clear notice, which also says warnings may be shared with family. Staying on the line is consent; hanging up is how a caller declines.

What the law says

PC § 632
Recording a confidential communication, including a phone call, requires the consent of all parties. A first offense can bring a fine of up to $2,500 and up to one year in jail.
PC § 632.7
Requires all-party consent to record calls involving a cell or cordless phone. Smith v. LoanMe (2021) applies this to people on the call, not just eavesdroppers, so Mary’s own phone needs consent too.
Kearney (2006)
The California Supreme Court held that telling all parties at the outset that the call will be recorded satisfies the law. That notice is what SafeCompanion plays.
Timing
Courts treat continuing after a clear, audible notice as implied consent, but consent given midway doesn’t cover what came before it. So recording starts only after the notice finishes.
PC § 637.2
A person recorded without consent can sue for $5,000 per violation or three times actual damages. Getting the notice right protects Mary from this.

How the design stays compliant

  • The notice plays before recording startsOn every call, incoming and outgoing, the other person hears who is recording and why.
  • Hanging up is a real way to declineIf the caller leaves at the notice, nothing is recorded. Scammers often do exactly this.
  • Mary always sees that she’s recordingA recording badge stays on screen for the whole call, and live scam signs explain why it’s useful.
  • Mary consents once, and can see it anytimeHer privacy screen states that calls are recorded with notice and who can play them.
  • Simple for Mary, detailed for familyMary sees one plain warning. Linda gets a live alert with only the flagged phrases, because Mary turned live alerts on in her settings. Neither sees a transcript.
  • Recorded doesn’t mean sharedOnly Mary can play recordings, and they’re deleted after 30 days. Linda can’t hear the call.
  • The law, in plain languageA big-type page tells Mary what callers hear and why it’s allowed.
  • Same rule in every state, and on merged callsAll-party consent is the default everywhere, and the notice replays when a new person joins.

How calls are sorted

Tier 1
Normal. No scam signs heard. Nobody is notified and the call isn’t shown to family, so alerts stay meaningful and everyday calls stay private.
Tier 2
Be careful. One scam sign. When the call ends, Mary gets a three-line summary and Linda gets the full summary with flagged phrases and next steps.
Tier 3
Likely scam. Two or more scam signs. Mary is warned during the call, Linda gets a live alert, and both get summaries afterward.

Questions to be ready for

Platforms
iPhone doesn’t give apps access to call audio, and Google Play restricts call recording through accessibility tools. A real build would likely route calls through the app (VoIP), a carrier partner, or a conference bridge.
Sharing
The notice tells callers warnings may reach family, and Linda gets short flagged phrases rather than the recording. Counsel should confirm that sharing excerpts stays within the consent the notice creates.
Personal calls
Implied consent from a notice is best established for business calls. Linda, doctors, and friends will hear it too, so counsel should confirm the wording and whether some calls need spoken consent.
