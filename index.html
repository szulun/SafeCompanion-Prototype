How recording is handled

Our design treats call recording as all-party consent. Before any recording starts, the caller hears a notice that the call may be recorded and analyzed to protect the person answering. We plan to confirm the exact wording with counsel before any real launch.

Points we are checking (not yet verified by counsel)

  • California Penal Code §632 and §632.7 require consent from all parties for recording a confidential call, including calls on cell phones.
  • Whether continuing after a notice counts as valid consent, and how Kearney v. Salomon Smith Barney (2006) and Smith v. LoanMe (2021) apply to this design.
  • Whether one-sided notices are enough for calls to people who are not the user.

What the prototype does

  • The notice plays before recording starts. If the caller hangs up, nothing is recorded.
  • Mary sees a visible recording badge during every call she answers through SafeCompanion.
  • Recordings can only be played by Mary and are deleted after 30 days (planned behavior; not implemented in the prototype).
  • Linda gets an automatic short note (possible scam) or real-time alert with the type only (likely scam). Details are shared only when Mary chooses. Mary can turn alerts off.

Sources: California Penal Code §§632, 632.7, 637.2. Case citations to be confirmed by counsel.
